## What requires separate permission

Commons Clause does not grant the right to **Sell** the software. Its definition
covers providing third parties, for a fee or other consideration (including fees
for hosting or consulting/support services related to the software), a product
or service whose value derives **entirely or substantially** from the software's
functionality. This test applies regardless of renaming or superficial changes.

The restriction is not a blanket ban on commercial activity, every commercial
integration or every paid service. Value-added products and integrations may be
permitted; see the [Commons Clause text and FAQ](https://commonsclause.com/).
Whether a particular offer satisfies the substantial-value test depends on its
facts. This guide cannot decide that in advance.

permission must be separately agreed. The public license creates **no automatic

commercial offer. Alternative permissions can cover only rights that the
licensor is authorized to grant, including any necessary contributor rights.
